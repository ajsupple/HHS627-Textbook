Matching Research Designs to Analyses

HHS 627

Choosing the Correct Statistical Test

Before selecting an analysis, ask:

  1. What is the research question?
  2. What is the hypothesis?
  3. What is the research design?
  4. How many variables are involved?
  5. What levels of measurement are present?

Everything starts with the research question, not the statistical test.

Research Questions Drive Analysis

Comparative Question

Do groups differ?

  • Are girls more likely than boys to report suicidal thoughts?
  • Do treatment participants differ from controls?

Associational Question

Are variables related?

  • Is discrimination associated with anxiety?
  • Is exercise intention associated with physical activity?

Comparative vs. Associational Designs

Comparative Designs Associational Designs
Compare groups Examine relationships
Categorical IV Continuous predictors
Mean differences Prediction and explanation
t-tests, ANOVA Correlation, Regression

Key Question

Do groups differ, or are variables related?

Internal Validity

Internal validity is the extent to which researchers can be confident that the proposed cause of an outcome is the actual cause.

High Internal Validity

  • Random assignment
  • Equivalent groups
  • Greater control

Low Internal Validity

  • Nonequivalent groups
  • Selection effects
  • Alternative explanations

Group Comparison Analyses

Research Situation Analysis
Two groups Independent-samples t test
Three or more groups ANOVA
Groups + control variables ANCOVA
Multiple outcomes MANOVA

Common Goal

Determine whether group means differ.

ANCOVA

Analysis of Covariance

ANOVA plus one or more covariates.

Example:

  • Compare depression groups on BDNF levels
  • Control for age

Purpose

Determine whether group differences remain after accounting for potential confounds.

Students often learn ANCOVA and regression separately, but they are mathematically very similar.

MANOVA

Multivariate Analysis of Variance

Used when there are multiple outcomes.

Example:

Compare groups on:

  • Depression
  • Anxiety
  • Stress

Advantages

  • Considers correlations among outcomes
  • Reduces multiple testing
  • Evaluates overall outcome patterns

Correlation Family

Correlation Type Variables
Pearson Continuous × Continuous
Spearman Ordered-Categorical
Kendall’s Tau Ordered-Categorical
Point-Biserial Continuous × Binary
Phi Binary × Binary

Core Question

How strongly are two variables related?

Multiple Regression

General Model

\[ Y = a + b_1X_1 + b_2X_2 + e \]

Researchers use regression to:

  • Predict outcomes
  • Control confounding variables
  • Estimate unique effects
  • Test theoretical models

Logistic Regression

Used when the outcome is binary.

Examples

  • Passed / Failed
  • Pregnant / Not Pregnant
  • Smoker / Non-Smoker
  • Survived / Died

Typical Results

  • Odds ratios
  • Confidence intervals
  • Predicted probabilities

Correlation vs. Regression

Correlation

  • Measures association
  • Usually involves two variables
  • No statistical control

Regression

  • Multiple predictors possible
  • Allows statistical control
  • Supports prediction

Key Idea

Regression often provides stronger evidence than a simple correlation.

The General Linear Model

Big Idea

Many statistical procedures are simply variations of the same framework.

  • t tests
  • ANOVA
  • ANCOVA
  • Multiple Regression

Important Point

ANOVA is a special case of regression.

Data Sources in HHS Research

Common sources include:

  • Self-report surveys
  • Observational data
  • Physiological measures
  • Administrative records
  • Public datasets
  • Wearable devices

Key Point

Data source often influences research design and statistical analysis.

Levels of Measurement

Hierarchy

Nominal → Ordinal → Interval → Ratio

Level Example
Nominal Gender, Race
Ordinal Likert Item
Interval Standardized Scores
Ratio Weight, Steps, Income

Choosing the Correct Analysis

Decision Process

  1. Comparative or associational question?
  2. How many groups?
  3. How many variables?
  4. What is the outcome type?
  5. What is the level of measurement?

Take-Home Message

Match the statistical analysis to the research design, not the other way around.

Discussion

Consider a study of college students:

  • Outcome: Depression
  • Predictor: Gender
  • Covariate: Body image
